## [Why hooks matter] The scroll happens before the story

A Reel does not begin when you introduce yourself. It begins at the first frame the viewer sees. They did not choose your video, so the opening has to earn the next second before the rest of the idea can work.

TikTok says the first six seconds are vital and reports that **90% of ad-recall impact** and **80% of awareness impact** can be captured within those six seconds. Its own Creative Center treats two-second and six-second view rates as core performance measures. Meta also advises brands to bring the story to life quickly and show the brand or product in the first few seconds.

These figures come from advertising research, so they are not a promise that an organic Reel will go viral. They do show why the opening deserves its own script, edit and measurement.

## [The job of a hook] What the first two seconds should do

### Identify the right viewer

Name the person, problem or situation immediately. “Carlow café owners” is clearer than “business owners.” “Your Reel loses viewers here” is clearer than “three content tips.” Specificity lets the right person recognise that the video is for them.

### Promise one useful payoff

Tell the viewer what they will see, learn or be able to do. The promise should fit the video that follows. A dramatic hook with a weak or unrelated payoff may earn a view, but it damages trust.

### Create a reason to wait

Leave one useful piece unresolved: the result, mistake, comparison, number or transformation. Curiosity works when the answer matters to the viewer, not when information is hidden for no reason.

### Show proof, action or change

Use the picture as part of the hook. Start with the finished cake, the surprising result, the mistake in progress or the strongest reaction. Do not spend the opening walking into the room or explaining what is about to happen.

### Make sense without sound

Use readable on-screen text to carry the core idea for viewers who start muted. TikTok found that ads showing on-screen text within the first seven seconds had a **43% conversion lift** compared with ads that waited longer.

### Introduce the brand naturally

Do not open with a large logo card. Let the product, person, location, colour or distinctive brand cue enter the action. TikTok research found that brand recognition within the first two seconds produced **57% more happiness** and **19% less attention decay**; the lesson is early recognition, not an interruption.

## [Measure the hook] The numbers to check before likes

Likes tell you how some viewers reacted after watching. They do not tell you how many people the opening lost. Start with retention and compare videos of similar length, topic and audience.

| Metric | Simple calculation | What it diagnoses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Two-second hold rate | Two-second views divided by video starts | Whether the first frame and opening line stopped the scroll |
| Six-second hold rate | Six-second views divided by video starts | Whether the promise stayed clear after the initial pattern break |
| Average percentage watched | Average watch time divided by video length | Whether the pacing and payoff held attention across the Reel |
| Completion rate | Full views divided by video starts | Whether viewers stayed for the ending; compare only similar video lengths |
| Rewatch signal | Views divided by accounts reached | Whether people replayed; useful for dense, surprising or satisfying videos |
| Shares and saves per reach | Shares plus saves divided by accounts reached | Whether the idea was useful or relevant enough to keep or pass on |

Instagram defines average watch time as total watch time divided by initial views, while its views can include replays. TikTok separately reports two-second views, six-second views, average play time and full views. Use each platform's own definitions when building your report instead of mixing numbers from different apps.

## [Working targets] A practical starting benchmark

There is no official universal “good hook rate.” Audience size, traffic source, topic and video length all change the result. The best benchmark is the median of your last ten comparable Reels. Until you have enough account data, use these as **working targets for testing**, not promises of reach.

| Metric | Starting target | How to read it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Two-second hold rate | 70% or more | Below this, test a stronger first frame, clearer text or a more specific opening line |
| Six-second hold rate | 45% or more | A large fall from two seconds usually means the hook promised more than the next shots delivered |
| Average percentage watched | 50% or more | If the hook is strong but this is weak, fix the middle rather than rewriting only the opening |
| Completion rate on a 15–30 second Reel | 30% or more | Treat length as part of the result; shorter videos naturally finish more often |
| Views per account reached | Above 1.05 | A value above one suggests some replaying, but check watch time before calling it a true rewatch win |

Raise the target when your ten-post median improves. A hook is successful when it beats your normal retention **and** brings the right viewers to the promised payoff.

## [Hook structures] Six openings worth testing

| Structure | Example opening | Why it can work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Specific problem | “Your café Reel loses customers in this frame.” | Names a relevant pain immediately |
| Result first | Show the finished transformation before the process | Proves that a payoff exists |
| Mistake first | “This is why your phone footage looks cheap.” | Creates tension and promises a correction |
| Named audience | “Carlow business owners: film this before Friday.” | Helps the right viewer self-select |
| Mid-action | Open while the cake drops, door opens or result is revealed | Removes setup and creates motion from frame one |
| Unexpected comparison | “The €20 setup beat the €2,000 camera.” | Creates a clear information gap with a concrete stake |

## [Testing] Change one variable, not the whole Reel

Make two or three openings for the same body edit. Change the first visual, spoken line and on-screen headline as one hook package, but keep the rest of the video identical. That gives you a cleaner comparison than changing the hook, length, audio and payoff at the same time.

Check the two-second rate first, then the six-second rate and average watch percentage. If the opening improves but the six-second line collapses, the hook is doing its job and the next section is not. If retention improves but shares, follows or enquiries do not, the hook may be attracting curiosity rather than the intended audience.

### The final rule

Do not write the most dramatic opening. Write the clearest compelling reason for the right person to watch the next second -- and deliver exactly what it promised.
