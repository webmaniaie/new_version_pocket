## [The problem] Nobody *chose* your reel.

A viewer did not open your video the way they open a film. It arrived. The only decision they make is whether to keep holding still, and they make it in about two seconds -- before they know the topic, the brand or the point.

So the hook is not the intro. It is the whole negotiation. Everything after it only matters to the people it kept.

![A strong visual opening prepared for a short-form video](assets/optimized/sb1.webp "portrait")

## [What a hook has to do] What the opening must achieve

### Make a promise worth waiting for

Give the viewer a clear reason to stay. The first image or sentence should tell them what they will understand, see or receive if they keep watching.

### Show that the payoff exists

Give a quick glimpse of the result without explaining all of it. The viewer should feel that the video is going somewhere specific.

### Leave one thing unresolved

Hold back the final answer, transformation or outcome. That open question gives the viewer a reason to watch the next second.

## [Four that keep working] Four useful hook structures {white}

### The wrong way first

Show the mistake before the correction. The contrast makes the lesson immediately clear.

### Mid-action open

Start at the interesting second instead of showing how the action began.

### Named audience

Say exactly who the video is for so the right viewer recognises themselves immediately.

### Visible stake

Show something on screen that can still succeed, fail or change before the reel ends.

## [Testing one] {white}

You do not need to reshoot to test a hook. Re-cut the same reel with a different first two seconds and post it a week apart. Compare the retention graph, not the likes: if the new opening holds people past the three second mark, keep it and move on to the next reel. One variable at a time, or you learn nothing.
